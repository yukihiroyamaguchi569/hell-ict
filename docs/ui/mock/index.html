Unexplained Febrile Cluster in Eastern Ling Province: Preliminary Report

                
Eastern Ling Infectious Disease Surveillance Office — Cluster Bulletin No. 18

                

                
Overview

                
Between 3 and 14 May, three hospitals in Eastern Ling Province reported 19 patients with fever and negative initial testing for common pathogens. Cases were identified through routine syndromic surveillance after the third hospital submitted its weekly summary. This preliminary report summarizes clinical records, laboratory findings, and follow-up interviews with patients and contacts collected through 20 May. It does not establish a causative agent, route of transmission, or common source.

                
Case Definition and Surveillance

                
A suspected case was defined as a person presenting with a documented temperature of at least 38.0°C and no alternative diagnosis confirmed within 48 hours. Surveillance staff at each hospital reviewed admission logs daily against this definition; no additional cases were found retrospectively among records from the preceding four weeks.

                
Descriptive Epidemiology

                
Patients ranged in age from 19 to 67 years (median 41); ten were male and nine female. Occupations were recorded for 17 patients, including four in healthcare, with no single workplace, school, or gathering reported by more than two patients.

                
Clinical Course

                
All 19 patients had a recorded temperature of at least 38.0°C at presentation, with fever duration ranging from two to six days. Fatigue was documented in 16 patients, headache in 13, and reduced appetite in 12; no patient reported cough, diarrhea, or rash.

                
Follow-up interviews found that 15 of 19 patients had experienced deep ocular pain 8 to 18 hours before fever onset, several describing a dull ache behind the eye that preceded any other symptom. Eleven patients described unusual sensitivity to light during the same pre-fever window. These symptoms were often recorded initially as eye strain or migraine and were not linked to fever in the original notes; the connection emerged only during structured interviews conducted after the cluster was recognized.

                
Laboratory Findings

                
Testing on all 19 patients included a respiratory viral panel, blood cultures, and complete blood count, with 11 also undergoing lumbar puncture; all panel and culture results were negative, and cerebrospinal fluid showed no pleocytosis where obtained. Extended cultures remain in progress at the provincial laboratory.

                
Timeline

                
Case counts rose through the second week, peaking at four patients on 11 May, and none have been reported since 16 May.

                
Contact Observations

                
Secondary illness was observed more often among people who had contact with a patient the day before that patient's fever began than among those whose contact occurred only afterward. Records of post-onset contact were incomplete, so this cannot determine whether pre-fever contact contributed to transmission.

                
Response Measures

                
Of an estimated 60 contacts identified, 38 were reached for interview; two facilities used cohort arrangements when single-room capacity ran short during the peak reporting week.

                
Limitations and Immediate Request

                
This is a small observational report drawn from three facilities over a twelve-day period and may not generalize beyond the reporting area. It remains unknown whether ocular symptoms indicate infection with the causative agent, whether the pre-fever contact association reflects transmission, or whether person-to-person transmission is occurring at all. Recall of pre-fever symptoms depended on interviews conducted after the fact and may be subject to recall bias. Healthcare facilities in Eastern Ling Province and neighboring jurisdictions are asked to record symptoms and contact history that precede unexplained fever, and to report any similar cluster to this office without delay.
